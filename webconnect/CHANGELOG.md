# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

### Changed (2026-11-05)

- **Pictures lay out by shape (portrait or landscape).** The viewer reads the frame's size and lays it out accordingly: wide frames fill the width and, when compared side by side, stack one above the other; tall frames are height-capped and centred, and two tall frames compared side by side sit next to each other. Applies to Camera, Focus and Solve. Style/markup only; not yet seen with a real portrait frame on a phone.
- **Pictures inside the pages that use them: Camera, Focus and Solve (`imageview.js`, delegated routes `frames`, `preview`, `preview/meta`, `tile`; `WCON-600`, `610`, `630`, `640`).** There is no separate Image tab. **Camera** embeds the full viewer: the Pier's current exposure or any recent Library frame as a JPEG the Master renders (the browser decodes no FITS and does no image processing), a choice of frame, the histogram, and **Blink** / **Side by side** comparison of two frames. **Focus** and **Solve** embed a compact picture (height-capped so the controls stay on screen) with its star count, HFR and FWHM under **Picture settings**. Every viewer follows new frames as they are captured, and offers clipping, detail (Fast / Balanced / Full, with size and quality capped at 2048 px and JPEG 92), binning, the frame's histogram and star statistics (count, HFR, FWHM), clipping, detail and binning, and **Zoom and pan** by 256-pixel tiles. While a session runs the Master renders smaller pictures and says so, so imaging is never slowed. Pictures are never cached by the browser (`no-store`), and the Master's cache avoids re-rendering. The night theme tints pictures red. This needs core's new frame sources (`PREV-070`); on an older Galileo the page says pictures are not available. A frame event no longer carries the imaging service object to browsers, and event filtering now matches a Pier whether its id arrives as text or a number (frame events were being dropped). When there is no current exposure yet a viewer opens on the newest Library frame, and if there is nothing at all it says so in place of a broken-image icon. Driven in headless Edge at phone size against the real preview code in three situations (a current exposure, Library frames only, nothing); picture, statistics, histogram, side by side, blink and 20 tiles all worked; **not yet with real hardware or a real phone**. Not done: FITS download (`WCON-620`), a live stack or guide/focus cut-out as a source, a spoke Pier's pictures (`WCON-210`), and a preview-limits option on the Options page (the limits are constants for now). Tests: `TC-WCON-300`, `600` (four cases), `610`, `630`; 57 in all.
- **Camera, Focuser, Guiding, Solve, Sessions and History pages (`camera.js`, `focuser.js`, `guiding.js`, `solve.js`, `sessions.js`, `history.js`, `common.js`; `WCON-400`, `800`).** Each uses the Mount page's look and the catalogue's own operations. **Camera**: sensor temperature and cooler power, a target-temperature setting, an exposure form (time, frame type, gain, offset, binning) with a progress bar fed by the event stream, Abort, and the filter wheel. **Focus**: position and temperature, nudge In/Out by 1 to 1000 steps, go to a position, HALT. **Guiding**: PHD2 connection and state, RA/Dec/total error, a live error trace chart, Connect, Find star, Start, Recalibrate, Dither, Stop, an optional guide-star image, and the PHD2 log. **Solve**: Solve, Solve and sync, Centre on a typed position with the solve settings tucked away, live progress and the last result (position, rotation, scale, attempts, log). **Session**: what is running with block and frame progress, Pause / Resume / Stop, starting a saved template (with an optional target), and the scheduler queue. **History**: recorded sessions, and for one its average HFR and star count with charts of HFR, stars and guiding error over its frames and a table of the latest frames. Controls grey out with the Master's reason when a device is missing or you do not hold control; dangerous or irreversible actions (sync, stop a session, start a session, solve and sync) ask first. The night theme keeps everything red, including the guide-star image. Operation progress events are now filtered by Pier. Checked by driving every page in a headless Edge at phone size against a fake Master (no script errors; dark and night screenshots reviewed); **not yet tried against real hardware, a real phone, or with a session running**. Not here: held focus motion, camera image preview (the catalogue has no image operation), editing the scheduler, and exporting history.
- **The web interface now looks and works like Galileo, starting with a Mount page as the template for the rest (`static/app.css`, `pier.js`, `mount.js`, `operations.js`; `WCON-400`, `800`).** The page uses the desktop's dark and light palettes and teal accent, plus a **night** theme in red only for a dark-adapted eye (the sun button in the header cycles them; the choice is remembered in the browser). Layout is touch-first: large tap targets, a bottom tab bar on a phone that becomes a left rail on a wide screen, and the page goes straight into the only (or last used) Pier, with the Pier name in the header to switch. A control bar shows whether you are viewing or hold control. **Mount** shows right ascension, declination, altitude, azimuth, sidereal time and pier side with Tracking / Slewing / Parked badges (refreshed every 2 s while the page is open), and offers a large **STOP**, a Tracking switch, Park / Unpark (parking asks again), and Slew to / Sync for coordinates typed as `hh:mm:ss` or decimals (sync asks first). **Status** (raw state and events) and **Operations** (every operation, built from the catalogue, so camera, guiding, solving and the rest stay reachable) are the other tabs. Not yet done: dedicated Camera, Focuser, Guiding, Solve, Sessions and History pages, manual slew buttons (the catalogue has no jog operation), the sky view, and the page has not been checked in a real browser or on a phone. Tests: `TC-WCON-800` (a second case; 50 total).

### Fixed (2026-11-04)

- **"Galileo's control surface is not running" gave no way forward.** The Control Surface that WebConnect operates exists only while Galileo hosts its engine, which needs Options > Equipment > "Hold devices in the device service" (off by default; takes effect at the next start). The browser's message and the Options > WebConnect status now say so.
- **Reloading the page returned to "Connect this device", and asking to connect then failed with "bad CSRF token".** After a reload the browser still held its session cookie but the page had lost its CSRF token, so the sign-in it sent was refused. `/signin` and `/pair/request` no longer demand the old session's token (they are same-origin checked and sign-in issues a fresh session), and the page first asks `/api/v1/me` for its token back, so a reload goes straight to the Piers. The Pier list now says why when it is empty (Galileo's own reason, or "no Piers to show yet") instead of showing nothing. Test: `TC-WCON-1040` (49 total).
- **"Galileo is not reachable" on every pairing attempt even with Connect Link running.** Connect Link listens on the computer's LAN address by default, but the web process was always told to reach it at `127.0.0.1`, where nothing listens. It is now given the address Connect Link actually bound (loopback only for a wildcard bind). Browsers that refuse the self-signed certificate no longer print a handshake traceback from the web process. Test: `TC-WCON-030` (one new case; 48 total).
- **A browser's certificate warning was shown as "Last error" (`failed with SSLError`).** When a browser refuses the self-signed certificate (or a plain `http://` request reaches the HTTPS port) the web server logs a handshake error for that connection; it is routine, so the supervisor no longer reports it.
- **"Address to open" showed `https://0.0.0.0:8443/` with all interfaces on.** `0.0.0.0` means "every interface" and is not an address a browser can visit. The page now shows the computer's LAN address, and the web application accepts requests addressed to it.
- **"Galileo is not reachable" when a browser asked to connect, with no hint why.** The web application reaches Galileo through Connect Link, which is off until switched on in Options > Connect Link; with it off the page loads but pairing cannot work. The WebConnect Options page now says "Connect Link is not listening. Turn it on in Options > Connect Link."
- **The packaged plugin answered every page with "Internal server error".** The plugin builder (`build_plugins.py`) zipped only `.py` and help files, so the installed plugin had no `web/templates` or `web/static` folders and the first page failed with `TemplateNotFound`. The builder now includes `templates/` and `static/` files (the zip has 6 more files), which also covers any other plugin that serves its own pages. The web process's output also now reaches the log and the Options page. Reinstall the rebuilt zip (`dist/galileo-plugin-webconnect-0.1.0.zip`) to pick this up; a development checkout was never affected, which is why the tests passed.
- **"Address to open" was blank and "failed to start N times in a row" gave no reason.** The address was shown only while the web process was alive, so during a crash loop it was always empty; it now always shows the address the application will listen on. The web process's error output was thrown away, so a start-up failure such as a missing `flask` or `gevent` was invisible; the supervisor now keeps the child's last error line and shows it on the Options page, in the log and in the "keeps failing" alert. The child's error lines are also written to Galileo's log and stay on the Options page while the application runs, so a page error (a 500) shows its cause. (The failure behind this report was exactly that: `gevent` was not installed in the environment, because dependencies are installed into `.deps` only when a plugin is installed from a package, not when it is loaded from a development checkout.)

### Added (2026-11-04)

- **The first operating screen (`static/pier.js`; `WCON-300`, `400`, `580`, `800`).** Choosing a Pier on the page opens its screen: a live state table that the event stream updates in place (and refetches after a `resync`), a control-lease bar (Take control / Release, Operators only; the Master decides), and every operation the Control Surface catalogue offers, grouped by area (guiding, solve, sky, history, mount...). The operation forms are built from the catalogue's argument schemas, so a new operation appears with no change to the page, an unavailable one is greyed with the Master's reason, a dangerous one asks again with the Master's confirmation token, and each press carries an idempotency key. When the stream drops, the header turns red and says the picture is the last known state. All markup is built with DOM calls, never from strings. Not yet tried in a real browser or on a tablet; the static sky image, held motion and previews are not here. Tests: `TC-WCON-800` (one new case; 47 total).
- **The Master half (`webconnect.plugin`, `.browsers`, `.delegate`, `.config`; `WCON-020`, `040`, `090`, `100`, `110`, `120`, `130`, `140`, `170`, `180`, `580`, `740`, `750`, `760`).** The plugin takes a place on Connect Link and registers the delegated routes under `/webconnect/v1`. A browser is an ordinary Connect Link peer: it pairs through core's own pairing (a code compared on both screens), its token becomes the device credential, and it is a **Viewer** until a person promotes it on the Master; no route can change a role. The web half's service credential names the browser it acts for, core refuses a browser that is unknown, revoked, expired or another plugin's, and every operation goes through the engine's shared request pipeline with the **browser's own** permissions, so lease, two-step confirmation, idempotent retry, validation and audit behave exactly as on the desktop. Revoking releases the browser's leases and ends its sessions. A node that has designated a NodeConnect Master (a spoke) starts nothing.
- **The web half (`webconnect.web`; `WCON-030`, `100`, `800`, `1000`, `1010`, `1020`, `1030`, `1050`, `1060`, `1490`).** A Flask application that holds no authority: it pairs and signs the browser in, keeps server-side sessions with a CSRF token, and relays every operation to the Master, which decides. Strict content-security-policy and related headers on every response, host and `Origin`/`Sec-Fetch-Site` checks, `__Host-` cookies (`HttpOnly`, `Secure`, `SameSite=Strict`), rate limits on pairing and sign-in, and a route registry that refuses to start if any route lacks an authentication class. When Galileo is not running the browser gets a plain page, not an error. A first page pairs the device and lists the Piers; the operating screens are not built yet.
- **Its own process, over TLS (`webconnect.supervisor`, `.tls`, `.launch`, `webconnect.web.__main__`; `WCON-030`, `040`, `060`, `070`, `910`).** The web half runs as `python -I` under gevent, configured only by one JSON line on its standard input (no secret in the command line, environment or a file), with a heartbeat back; a dead or silent process is restarted with a growing delay and reported after repeated failures, and it exits if the Master half goes away. It has its own self-signed certificate, with the key in the secret store, replaceable by the user's own pair. It binds the LAN interface, never every interface unless explicitly allowed.
- **Options > WebConnect page and inline help (`webconnect.ui`, `help/plugin.WebConnect.md`; `WCON-050`, `100`, `110`, `170`, `190`, `900`).** Shows the address to open, starts and stops the web application, and keeps the port, start-with-Galileo, all-interfaces and idle-timeout options (saving restarts a running web application). Pairing requests appear with their codes for **Approve** or **Reject**; approved browsers can be made Operator or Viewer, given an expiry, revoked or removed; **Recent activity** lists what browsers asked for, refusals included. Every control has a help entry and the help audit is clean. Not yet shown: signed-in browsers and the Piers they hold a lease on.
- **Live updates (`GET /webconnect/v1/events` on the Master, `GET /api/v1/events` for browsers; `WCON-300`, `310`, `350`).** The Master half serves core's new ordered event feed (state, lease, device and operation events) to a named browser after a cursor, waiting up to 8 s for news on a worker thread so it never holds up Connect Link. The web half turns that into Server-Sent Events: a `hello`, then each change carrying an id (so a dropped browser resumes from `Last-Event-ID`), `resync` when the feed dropped entries the browser needed, `down` when Galileo is unreachable or shutting down, and `signed-out` when the browser is revoked mid-stream. A sign-in may hold four streams open, and an open stream does not count as activity, so a forgotten tab still signs out on schedule.
- **Fixed before it could bite: the web process never yielded to other browsers.** It served with gevent but did not patch sockets, so one slow call to the Master stalled every connected browser for its duration, and a long poll would have stalled them all. The child now patches sockets at boot (threads stay real, since the stdin reader and heartbeat block on a pipe). A test starts six browsers against a Master that takes 0.5 s per call: 3.0 s unpatched, about 0.5 s patched.
- **Tests:** 46, among them a real `python -I` child serving HTTPS and leaving no listener after shutdown, the web half driven end to end against the real Master half over an in-process bridge, and the web half's pinned HTTPS client against core's real Connect Link listener (pairing, sign-in, lease, an operation, revocation, a wrong certificate and a wrong credential).
